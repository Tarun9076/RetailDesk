# Scope and assumptions

## In scope (core build)
- Retailer: SMS OTP login, catalogue (retailer price, GST shown, stock status, scheme), cart, place order, order history, repeat order
- Scheme engine: free goods (buy N get M) and slab percent discounts
- AI order capture from a text message (Gemini), with a human review step
- Order desk: email + password login, order queue, credit holds, Tally export
- Hindi and English UI

## Cut (and why)
- Voice-note capture: same parser interface, extra input type (photo of a list is built)
- Salesman mode and offline sync: needs a sync layer, not part of the core slice
- 14-day no-order dashboard: built last if time allows
- Real payments, delivery tracking, GST e-invoicing: out of scope in the brief

## Assumptions (to be repeated in the TDD)
- A1. Dealer price in the sample data is per piece; case price = price x pieces per case (biscuit 96, detergent 20, tea 40).
- A2. Sample prices are GST-exclusive; GST is added at 12% (biscuit, tea) and 18% (detergent). Rates are invented.
- A3. Orders are in whole cases only; free goods are whole cases.
- A4. A product can carry several schemes (for example 3% at 5+ cases and 5% at 20+ cases, or a free-goods rule beside a slab). Only one applies to a line, the one worth most to the shop at that quantity; schemes never stack. Free goods and a percentage are compared in the same units (a free case is worth a case price), so no price is needed. On an exact tie a percentage wins, then the lower threshold, then the label, so the same rows always give the same answer. Free goods: floor(qty / buyQty) x freeQty, repeating for every full block. A slab applies to the whole line once qty >= minQty. The "add N more cases" hint only suggests a threshold that would really win at that quantity.
- A35. A scheme's "valid from" and "valid until" are whole Indian days: it starts at 00:00 IST on its first day and runs to the end of its last day.
- A5. Free cases must be in stock too; an order cannot exceed available stock.
- A6. Credit check: outstanding + order total > credit limit puts the order ON_HOLD for the desk.
- A7. Retailer-specific prices are a per-SKU override; otherwise the dealer price applies.
- A8. Retailers, outstanding balances, credit limits and orders are invented seed data.
- A9. OTP via SMS: the app makes and checks its own six-digit code (hashed, five minutes, single use) and sends it with Twilio's plain SMS API. Seeded demo numbers accept a fixed test code, enabled only by env var.
- A11. Credit used = ledger outstanding + orders not yet exported to Tally (placed or confirmed). Orders already on hold are not counted. Over-limit orders are held for the desk, not rejected.
- A12. Stock is reserved when the order is placed (paid + free cases) and is put back if the desk cancels the order.
- A13. When the desk exports an order to Tally, the shop's account balance goes up by the order total. Tally is the real ledger, so this mirrors what it will show after import, and keeps "credit used" correct once the order leaves the queue.
- A14. The Tally file assumes intra-state sales (Jabalpur shops): GST is booked as half CGST and half SGST, per rate. Free goods are zero-value stock lines. Ledger names (Sales, Output CGST @6%, ...) and the stock unit "Case" are placeholders the client must confirm.
- A15. The desk can release a credit hold on its own judgement. There is no second approver, but every release records who did it and when. Exported and cancelled orders are final.
- A16. The desk screens are English only. The language switch is for shopkeepers.
- A17. AI capture reads typed text and photos of a list (handwritten or printed). A Hindi voice note would use the same pipeline (turned into text first) but is not built. The photo is sent to the AI and not stored; only the words read from it are kept. The AI reads the message into lines and may suggest up to two product codes as a hint; the app's own matcher makes the final choice and scores it.
- A18. The shop is only ever shown cases. If a message says pieces, the quantity is converted to whole cases and rounded up, and the shopkeeper sees that. A missing quantity or unit is asked, never assumed.
- A19. Matching uses only that shop's own past orders and learned phrases. What one shop teaches the app never affects another shop.
- A20. A correction is remembered when the shopkeeper (or desk) changes a guess, or picks on a line the app was unsure about. A swap away from an out-of-stock product is treated as a substitution and is not remembered.
- A21. The desk can change a line's product on an order that is still being checked (to check or on hold). The order is priced again and the credit check re-run. Confirmed, exported and cancelled orders cannot be changed.
- A22. Confidence is shown as three words (sure, please check, not sure), not a percentage, because the number is not a calibrated probability.
- A23. "No order in 14 days" is counted in Indian calendar days since the shop's latest order that was not cancelled. Exactly 14 days counts as quiet. Shops that have never ordered are listed separately, at the end.
- A24. The WhatsApp button only opens a pre-written message in WhatsApp on the desk user's own device. Nothing is sent by the app, because messaging is mocked for this build.
- A25. No web fonts: the app uses each phone's own system fonts (Roboto/Noto on Android, San Francisco on iPhone, Segoe UI/Nirmala on Windows). Measured on a production build, Noto Sans added about 300 KB to every first load.
- A26. Offline use is limited to a friendly offline page and cached static files. Prices, credit and orders are never cached on the phone. Salesman offline ordering with sync is not built.
- A27. "Sales" on the dashboard means orders the desk has confirmed or exported, by order date in Indian time, after scheme discounts and before GST. Orders to check or on hold are shown as "waiting", and cancelled orders count for nothing. The comparison is with the same number of days just before.
- A28. The Excel sales register is the main export. It is a register for the accounts team to read and enter or reconcile, not a Tally import: Tally Prime imports reliably from XML, which stays available as a secondary option. Both use the same GST split (half CGST, half SGST per rate).
- A29. The dashboard's AI share counts every order placed in the period that was not cancelled, so a message order counts as soon as it is placed. Every order here is placed in the app, so the client's "50% in the app or by AI" goal is tracked by the AI share, the stricter half.
- A30. "Download app" installs the web app from the browser (no app store). It uses the browser's own install dialog where one is offered, and shows step-by-step instructions where it is not (for example on iPhone).
- A31. Charts use a brighter green than the brand green because the brand green failed the colour validator's chroma check for data marks. The app is light only, so charts have no dark mode.
- A32. "Responsive" means the layout changes with the device (columns, tables, sticky panels), not only that nothing overflows. Breakpoints: 360 px (one product per row below it), 420 px (Sign out gets a text label), 640 px (tablet-style headers), 768 px (two-column grids), 1024 px (two-column cart and order detail, desk table), 1280 px (five products per row).
- A33. Measured with a script in a real browser at 320, 375, 414, 768, 1024, 1280 and 1920 px, not on physical devices. Real phones, especially iPhones in Safari, may differ in small ways (for example the notch and install steps) and should be tried before launch.
- A34. The desk is designed for a laptop or monitor first, and works on a tablet or phone for quick checks (approving a hold, glancing at sales). Printing the loading list is a laptop task.
- A36. Each shop has at most one salesman, assigned by the desk (any number of shops per salesman). A salesman logs in with their own phone number and the same SMS code flow as shops. Their session lasts 7 days (shopkeepers 30 days, desk 12 hours) because the phone holds shop prices.
- A37. A salesman sees, and may order for, only their own shops, with each shop's own prices and credit. This is enforced on the server (the sync package, and the order API refuses another salesman's shop with 403), not only in the screens. Retailer prices stay visible only to that retailer, their salesman and the desk.
- A38. A salesman's order is the shop's order: it is priced, checked for credit and put in the desk queue exactly like one the shop placed, and it counts as that shop's order for the 14-day "no order" list. It records the salesman who took it, and the phone's time if it was taken offline.
- A39. The salesman app is in English and Hindi, with one button in its header that flips between them. The language switch works on the phone itself, so it works offline too, and it uses the same saved choice as the shop screens. Product names, schemes and hints follow it, and so do dates and the messages the office sends back (for example "only 5 cases can be supplied"). Hindi uses the word "पेटी" for a case, as the shop screens do. The desk stays English only.
- A40. Offline: the phone keeps the product list, each shop's price differences, stock, schemes and credit as of the last sync, in its own database. Orders are queued there and sent when possible. The server's price always wins, and the salesman is shown any difference. Stock is not reserved while offline, so an order can be refused for stock on arrival. Orders sent more than three days ago are removed from the phone's list. Salesman AI capture (paste a message) and voice or photo capture are not offered.
- A41. Signing out on a shared phone deletes shop data and carts but keeps unsent orders, hidden from other users. Offline sign-out clears the data immediately and the cookie later.
- A42. Only the desk creates accounts. There is no self sign-up for salesmen or shops, because an account gives access to prices and credit. The desk adds a person by name and mobile number, and they sign in with an SMS code. A mobile number can belong to one account only (shop or salesman), so the sign-in screen never has to guess.
- A43. A salesman who leaves is switched off, not deleted. They are signed out on their next request, cannot sign in, and their shops become unassigned. History is kept. A new shop starts with the dealer prices; per-shop prices are not yet editable in the app.
- A44. The desk manages the catalogue in the app. A product code is unique and cannot change once created. Products are never deleted, only taken off sale, because past orders refer to them. An off-sale product disappears from shops and salesmen and cannot be ordered, but past orders are untouched.
- A45. Every stock change needs a reason and is recorded with the desk user's name. Adding and removing are single atomic updates, so an order arriving at the same moment is never lost. "Set to a count" is refused if the stock moved since the desk looked (an order came in), instead of overwriting it. Stock cannot go below zero.
- A46. The desk writes a scheme as numbers (buy and free cases, or minimum cases and percentage) and the English wording is generated from them, so the words can never disagree with the rule. A scheme can start and end on chosen days (whole Indian days). The same rule cannot be added twice while switched on. Removing a scheme does not change past orders, which keep the scheme name they were priced with.
- A47. A new price or scheme applies to the next order that is placed. A cart already open on a phone is priced again by the server when the order is placed, and a salesman's phone picks up changes at its next sync. GST rates are chosen from 0, 5, 12, 18 and 28 percent.
- A48. A shop can be switched off, never deleted: past orders refer to it. Switching off (or changing the mobile number) signs the owner out at once, stops sign-in and ordering, and removes the shop from its salesman's phone. Orders it already has stay in the queue for the desk to finish. Two shops cannot share a mobile number or a Tally name.
- A49. Desk staff are added by another staff member, never by self sign-up. The new account gets a temporary password shown once; it can only open the "choose your own password" screen until changed. Passwords need 10+ characters with letters and numbers (or 16+) and cannot be common, the email or the old password. A forgotten password is reset by a colleague, or from the command line if nobody can sign in. Changing or resetting a password signs out that person's other logins; the last active desk account can never be switched off, and nobody can switch themselves off.
- A50. A shop's "amount owed" is edited by the desk on the shop screen and raised by exported orders. Reading it back from Tally (an import screen and a nightly script) was built, then taken out for now: the client said it is not needed yet.
- A51. The names the Tally file uses are the defaults in `src/lib/tally/voucher.ts`, with `TALLY_COMPANY` for the company. A settings screen and a checklist were built, then taken out for now; the client confirms the real names at the first trial import.
- A52. Server errors are grouped by message, route and method, with ids and numbers flattened. Emails, phone numbers, tokens and database addresses are removed before anything is stored or sent. An alert is sent for a new problem, and again after one marked as dealt with comes back; a repeat is mentioned at most once an hour. Request bodies and headers are never kept.
- A53. Backups are the owner's job to keep and protect. `npm run db:backup` makes a full copy (with password hashes) and `npm run db:restore` loads it into an empty database only. A desk download was taken out for now.
- A54. Product pictures are drawn in code from the product's own name and brand (carton, pouch, bottle, tub or soap bar), so they cost no download and a new product has a picture at once. A real photo, as an https address entered on the desk's product page, replaces the drawing. Photos are not uploaded or stored by the app: the desk hosts them, and a photo that fails to load is replaced by the drawing. Photos do not work offline for salesmen; the drawing does.
- A55. The catalogue card shows the price first, then the name, GST, piece price, scheme and (only when low or out) stock. "In stock" is not written for the many products that are. The long name is cut at the first comma ("Cream biscuit 200 g, case of 48" shows as "Cream biscuit 200 g"). Whole-rupee case prices drop ".00" on the card (₹1,440); bills and totals always show paise.
- A56. The design keeps one accent colour. Green is for actions and good news; the selected filter is dark. Sign out and Download app sit in a menu. The salesman app shares the product card, the cart bar and the quantity bar, and keeps its own header because it also carries the sync status.
- A10. Tally export is a Tally Prime sales-voucher XML; the client's real ledger and item names are unknown.
